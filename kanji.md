###  The Mathematics of the Ancients (Numbers)

*Notice how the first three numbers are pure logic (tally marks), but as numbers get higher, ancient scholars had to borrow symbols for abstract sounds or use philosophical concepts like "dividing" and "completeness."*

**1. 一 (One)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pure **ideograph**. It is a single horizontal line representing the absolute concept of "one" or a single finger raised. The foundation of all counting.
*   **Onyomi:** イチ (ichi) | **Kunyomi:** ひと.つ (hito.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 一 (いち - one), 一つ (ひとつ - one thing)
*   **Example:** りんごを一つください。(りんごを ひとつ ください。 - Please give me one apple.)

**2. 二 (Two)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pure **ideograph**. Two horizontal lines. In ancient philosophy, it also represented the duality of heaven (top line) and earth (bottom line).
*   **Onyomi:** ニ (ni) | **Kunyomi:** ふた.つ (futa.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 二 (に - two), 二人 (ふたり - two people)
*   **Example:** ここに二人がいます。(ここに ふたりが います。 - There are two people here.)

**3. 三 (Three)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pure **ideograph**. Three lines representing the triad of ancient philosophy: Heaven (top), Earth (bottom), and Humanity (middle).
*   **Onyomi:** サン (san) | **Kunyomi:** みっ.つ (mit.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 三 (さん - three), 三日 (みっか - 3rd day)
*   **Example:** 三日間に休みます。(みっかかん やすみます。 - I will rest for three days.)

**4. 四 (Four)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally written as four horizontal lines (亖), but this caused visual confusion. The current shape is a box (囗) with "breath" or dividing lines (八) inside. It symbolizes the four cardinal directions (North, South, East, West) contained within the physical world.
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** よん (yon), よ (yo), よっ.つ (yot.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 四 (よん - four), 四月 (しがつ - April)
*   **Example:** ペンが四本あります。(ぺんが よんほん あります。 - There are four pens.)

**5. 五 (Five)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally an "X" shape drawn between heaven and earth (two horizontal lines). The "X" represents "crossing" or intersecting, as 5 marks the exact halfway point in the base-10 counting system.
*   **Onyomi:** ゴ (go) | **Kunyomi:** いつ.つ (itsu.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 五 (ご - five), 五時 (ごじ - 5 o'clock)
*   **Example:** 今は五時です。(いまは ごじ です。 - It is 5 o'clock now.)

**6. 六 (Six)**
*   **Historical Origin:** This is an anthropological phonetics trick! It is actually a drawing of a **simple hut or tent**. Because the ancient spoken word for "hut" sounded exactly like the spoken word for "six," they borrowed the picture to write the number.
*   **Onyomi:** ロク (roku) | **Kunyomi:** むっ.つ (mut.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 六 (ろく - six), 六百 (ろっぴゃく - 600)
*   **Example:** 卵を六つ買いました。(たまごを むっつ かいました。 - I bought six eggs.)

**7. 七 (Seven)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally drawn as a cross (+), depicting a horizontal bone or stick being cleanly sliced by a vertical knife. Like "six," it was a phonetic loan where the word for "cut" sounded like the word for "seven."
*   **Onyomi:** シチ (shichi) | **Kunyomi:** なな (nana), なな.つ (nana.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 七 (なな - seven), 七時 (しちじ - 7 o'clock)
*   **Example:** 毎朝七時に起きます。(まいあさ しちじに おきます。 - I wake up at 7 o'clock every morning.)

**8. 八 (Eight)**
*   **Historical Origin:** An ideograph of two lines turning away from each other. It visually represents "splitting" or "dividing." Because 8 is the ultimate divisible number (8 -> 4 -> 2 -> 1), it became the symbol for eight.
*   **Onyomi:** ハチ (hachi) | **Kunyomi:** やっ.つ (yat.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 八 (はち - eight), 八百 (はっぴゃく - 800)
*   **Example:** りんごが八つあります。(りんごが やっつ あります。 - There are eight apples.)

**9. 九 (Nine)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Another phonetic loan. It is a pictograph of a bending arm or a hooked tool. It was borrowed purely because the sound matched the spoken word for "nine."
*   **Onyomi:** キュウ (kyuu), ク (ku) | **Kunyomi:** ここの.つ (kokono.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 九 (きゅう - nine), 九時 (くじ - 9 o'clock)
*   **Example:** 夜九時に寝ます。(よる くじに ねます。 - I sleep at 9 PM.)

**10. 十 (Ten)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally a single vertical line used to count tens, which later got a dot in the middle, stretching into a cross. It points in all four directions and the center, representing numerical completeness and totality.
*   **Onyomi:** ジュウ (juu) | **Kunyomi:** とお (too)
*   **N5 Words:** 十 (じゅう - ten), 十分 (じゅっぷん - 10 minutes)
*   **Example:** 傘が十本あります。(かさが じゅっぽん あります。 - There are ten umbrellas.)

**11. 百 (Hundred)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A combination of 一 (one) and 白 (white). Ancient scholars believe 白 was a picture of a thumb or a pinecone. It was used phonetically to mean "one hundred."
*   **Onyomi:** ヒャク (hyaku) | **Kunyomi:** (none commonly used at N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 百 (ひゃく - 100), 三百 (さんびゃく - 300)
*   **Example:** この本は三百円です。(この ほんは さんびゃく えん です。 - This book is 300 yen.)

**12. 千 (Thousand)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A combination of 人 (person) and a horizontal line (一). They took the concept of a person and modified it to represent the massive, abstract scale of a thousand.
*   **Onyomi:** セン (sen) | **Kunyomi:** ち (chi)
*   **N5 Words:** 千 (せん - 1000), 三千 (さんぜん - 3000)
*   **Example:** 千円を貸してください。(せんえんを かして ください。 - Please lend me 1000 yen.)

**13. 万 (Ten Thousand)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The traditional form (萬) was an intricate drawing of a scorpion! Borrowed for its sound. The modern form (万) derives from an ancient swastika-like symbol representing "the universe," "all directions," or essentially infinity, fitting for such a huge number.
*   **Onyomi:** マン (man), バン (ban) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 一万 (いちまん - 10,000)
*   **Example:** あの車は百万円です。(あの くるまは ひゃくまんえん です。 - That car is one million yen.)

###  Cosmic Cycles, Elements & Economy

*The ancients looked to the sky to track time and the earth for wealth and survival. Notice how these physical elements form the days of the week.*

**14. 円 (Yen / Circle)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The traditional form (圓) is a picture of a round cauldron surrounded by a circle. Its absolute meaning is "round." Because metal coins are round, it evolved to mean currency.
*   **Onyomi:** エン (en) | **Kunyomi:** まる.い (maru.i)
*   **N5 Words:** 円 (えん - Yen), 百円 (ひゃくえん - 100 Yen)
*   **Example:** 水は百円です。(みずは ひゃくえん です。 - The water is 100 yen.)

**15. 時 (Time / Hour)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **日 (sun)**. The right is **寺 (temple / government office)**. In the ancient world, time wasn't kept on wrists; it was tracked by officials in temples observing the sun and striking a bell for the community.
*   **Onyomi:** ジ (ji) | **Kunyomi:** とき (toki)
*   **N5 Words:** 時計 (とけい - watch/clock), 時々 (ときどき - sometimes)
*   **Example:** 今、何時ですか。(いま、なんじ ですか。 - What time is it now?)

**16. 日 (Sun / Day)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A direct pictograph of the sun. The dot/line in the middle indicates it is a solid, glowing mass (or represents a sunspot!). Because the sun's cycle defines our days, it means both "sun" and "day."
*   **Onyomi:** ニチ (nichi), ジツ (jitsu) | **Kunyomi:** ひ (hi), か (ka)
*   **N5 Words:** 毎日 (まいにち - everyday), 日本 (にほん - Japan = *origin of the sun*)
*   **Example:** 毎日日本語を勉強します。(まいにち にほんごを べんきょう します。 - I study Japanese every day.)

**19. 月 (Moon / Month)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a crescent moon. The inner lines represent craters/shadows. Since ancient calendars were lunar, one cycle of this celestial body equals one "month."
*   **Onyomi:** ゲツ (getsu), ガツ (gatsu) | **Kunyomi:** つき (tsuki)
*   **N5 Words:** 月曜日 (げつようび - Monday), 今月 (こんげつ - this month)
*   **Example:** 月曜日に学校へ行きます。(げつようびに がっこうへ いきます。 - I go to school on Monday.)

**20. 火 (Fire)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a campfire. You can see a central burning log with three flames licking upward.
*   **Onyomi:** カ (ka) | **Kunyomi:** ひ (hi)
*   **N5 Words:** 火曜日 (かようび - Tuesday), 火山 (かざん - volcano)
*   **Example:** 火曜日は暇です。(かようびは ひま です。 - I am free on Tuesday.)

**21. 水 (Water)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A top-down pictograph of a flowing river. The long central line is the main current, and the outer strokes are splashes, eddies, or riverbanks.
*   **Onyomi:** スイ (sui) | **Kunyomi:** みず (mizu)
*   **N5 Words:** 水曜日 (すいようび - Wednesday), 水 (みず - water)
*   **Example:** 水を飲みます。(みずを のみます。 - I drink water.)

**22. 木 (Tree / Wood)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A beautifully simple pictograph. The horizontal line is the branches, the vertical line is the trunk, and the bottom swoops are the roots gripping the earth.
*   **Onyomi:** モク (moku), ボク (boku) | **Kunyomi:** き (ki)
*   **N5 Words:** 木曜日 (もくようび - Thursday), 木 (き - tree)
*   **Example:** 木の下に猫がいます。(きの したに ねこが います。 - There is a cat under the tree.)

**23. 金 (Gold / Money)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (𠆢) represents a mountain. The horizontal lines represent layers of earth/soil. The two dots are **nuggets of gold** hidden deep underground. Thus, it represents precious metals and wealth.
*   **Onyomi:** キン (kin) | **Kunyomi:** かね (kane)
*   **N5 Words:** 金曜日 (きんようび - Friday), お金 (おかね - money)
*   **Example:** 今日はお金がありません。(きょうは おかねが ありません。 - I have no money today.)

**24. 土 (Soil / Earth)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a clump or mound of soil (the cross shape) sitting on top of the flat ground (the bottom horizontal line). 
*   **Onyomi:** ド (do), ト (to) | **Kunyomi:** つち (tsuchi)
*   **N5 Words:** 土曜日 (どようび - Saturday)
*   **Example:** 土曜日に映画を見ます。(どようびに えいがを みます。 - I will watch a movie on Saturday.)

**25. 曜 (Weekday / Shining Celestial Body)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **日 (sun)**. The right (翟) is a pheasant, but here it acts as a phonetic meaning "dazzling/shining." It originally referred to the 7 "shining bodies" of the sky (Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn), which mapped perfectly to the 7-day week!
*   **Onyomi:** ヨウ (you) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 曜日 (ようび - day of the week)
*   **Example:** 今日は何曜日ですか。(きょうは なんようび ですか。 - What day of the week is it today?)

###  Philosophy of Space & Humanity

*How do you draw a concept you cannot see? The ancients used baselines, arrows, and roots to map out abstract realities.*

**17. 本 (Basis / Book / Origin)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the tree kanji (**木**). Now, draw a solid horizontal line right across the bottom (**本**). That line acts as an indicator pointing to the **roots**. "Roots" evolved to mean "origin/foundation." Because ancient books were made of wood/bamboo slips, it also came to mean "book."
*   **Onyomi:** ホン (hon) | **Kunyomi:** もと (moto)
*   **N5 Words:** 本 (ほん - book), 日本 (にほん - Japan = *Origin of the sun*)
*   **Example:** 私は本を読みます。(わたしは ほんを よみます。 - I read a book.)

**18. 人 (Person)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A side-profile drawing of a human being. They are standing on two legs with their back slightly bent and arms reaching forward (as if walking or working).
*   **Onyomi:** ジン (jin), ニン (nin) | **Kunyomi:** ひと (hito)
*   **N5 Words:** 人 (ひと - person), 日本人 (にほんじん - Japanese person)
*   **Example:** あの人は誰ですか。(あの ひとは だれ ですか。 - Who is that person?)

**26. 上 (Up / Above)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pure **ideograph**. It was originally a long horizontal baseline, with a shorter line sitting on top of it, pointing upward. It is a visual map of the concept of "above."
*   **Onyomi:** ジョウ (jou) | **Kunyomi:** うえ (ue), あ.がる (a.garu)
*   **N5 Words:** 上 (うえ - above/on), 上手 (じょうず - skillful = *having the "upper hand"*)
*   **Example:** 机の上にペンがあります。(つくえの うえに ぺんが あります。 - There is a pen on the desk.)

**27. 下 (Down / Below)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The philosophical opposite of 上. A long horizontal baseline, with a shorter line hanging below it. It is a visual map of "under."
*   **Onyomi:** カ (ka), ゲ (ge) | **Kunyomi:** した (shita), さ.がる (sa.garu)
*   **N5 Words:** 下 (した - under/below), 下手 (へた - unskillful = *having the "lower hand"*)
*   **Example:** ベッドの下に靴があります。(べっどの したに くつが あります。 - There are shoes under the bed.)

**28. 中 (Middle / Inside)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A highly kinetic ideograph. You have a square target (囗). Slashing right through the exact center of it is an arrow (丨). It represents hitting the "middle," penetrating the "inside," or finding the "center."
*   **Onyomi:** チュウ (chuu) | **Kunyomi:** なか (naka)
*   **N5 Words:** 中 (なか - inside), 一日中 (いちにちじゅう - all day long)
*   **Example:** かばんの中に本があります。(かばんの なかに ほんが あります。 - There is a book inside the bag.)

### Agriculture & Society (Notice the connections!)

**29. 私 (Private / I / Me)**
*   **Historical Origin:** You will love this one based on our last conversation! The left side is **禾 (grain)**. The right side is **厶 (a bent arm pulling toward oneself)**. In an agricultural society where grain is money, pulling grain towards yourself meant "private property" or "my own." This evolved into the pronoun "I" or "me."
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** わたし (watashi)
*   **N5 Words:** 私 (わたし - I/me)
*   **Example:** 私は学生です。(わたしは がくせい です。 - I am a student.)

**30. 気 (Spirit / Energy / Atmosphere)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The traditional form is **氣**. The top part (气) is a picture of **rising vapor or steam**. What is inside? **米 (rice)**! The ancient Chinese believed the invisible energy of the universe (Qi/Ki) was like the nourishing steam rising from a hot pot of cooking rice. Food equals life force.
*   **Onyomi:** キ (ki) | **Kunyomi:** (none at N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 元気 (げんき - healthy/energetic), 天気 (てんき - weather), 病気 (びょうき - illness - *N4 kanji but N5 word*)
*   **Example:** 今日はいい天気です。(きょうは いい てんき です。 - The weather is good today.)

**31. 田 (Rice Field)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pure pictograph of a plot of farming land viewed from above, divided by irrigation ditches.
*   **Onyomi:** デン (den) | **Kunyomi:** た (ta), だ (da)
*   **N5 Words:** 田 (た - rice field), 山田さん (やまださん - Mr./Ms. Yamada)
*   **Example:** 山田さんは日本人です。(やまださんは にほんじん です。 - Mr. Yamada is Japanese.)

**32. 男 (Man / Male)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A socio-historical compound. The top is **田 (rice field)** and the bottom is **力 (power/strength/a plow)**. In ancient society, a "man" was defined by his role: providing the physical labor/power in the rice fields. 
*   **Onyomi:** ダン (dan), ナン (nan) | **Kunyomi:** おとこ (otoko)
*   **N5 Words:** 男の人 (おとこの ひと - man), 男の子 (おとこの こ - boy)
*   **Example:** あの男の人は先生です。(あの おとこの ひとは せんせい です。 - That man is a teacher.)

**33. 女 (Woman / Female)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a person kneeling down, viewed from the side, with arms crossed in front. It reflects the submissive or domestic posture expected of women in ancient patriarchal Chinese society.
*   **Onyomi:** ジョ (jo) | **Kunyomi:** おんな (onna)
*   **N5 Words:** 女の人 (おんなの ひと - woman), 女の子 (おんなの こ - girl)
*   **Example:** 女の子が三人います。(おんなの こが さんにん います。 - There are three girls.)

**34. 半 (Half)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top two strokes **丷 (eight/divide)** sit above **牛 (cow)**. It depicts a cow being physically split down the middle. This refers to ancient ritual animal sacrifice, where livestock was cut in half for offerings.
*   **Onyomi:** ハン (han) | **Kunyomi:** (none at N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 半分 (はんぶん - half), 五時半 (ごじはん - 5:30)
*   **Example:** りんごを半分ください。(りんごを はんぶん ください。 - Please give me half an apple.)

### Nature & The Human Body

**35. 山 (Mountain)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A literal drawing of three mountain peaks.
*   **Onyomi:** サン (san) | **Kunyomi:** やま (yama)
*   **N5 Words:** 山 (やま - mountain), 富士山 (ふじさん - Mt. Fuji), 火山 (かざん - volcano)
*   **Example:** 山に行きます。(やまに いきます。 - I will go to the mountain.)

**36. 川 (River)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A drawing of flowing water. The two outer lines are the riverbanks, and the middle line is the current.
*   **Onyomi:** セン (sen) | **Kunyomi:** かわ (kawa), がわ (gawa)
*   **N5 Words:** 川 (かわ - river), 山川さん (やまかわさん - Mr./Ms. Yamakawa)
*   **Example:** 川に魚がいます。(かわに さかなが います。 - There are fish in the river.)

**37. 天 (Heaven / Sky)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the kanji for "big" or "person" (大) and draw a line (一) right above it. It literally represents the infinite expanse physically resting *above* humanity. 
*   **Onyomi:** テン (ten) | **Kunyomi:** あまつ (amatsu - rare)
*   **N5 Words:** 天気 (てんき - weather), 天国 (てんごく - heaven - *N4 kanji but good to know*)
*   **Example:** 天気が悪いです。(てんきが わるい です。 - The weather is bad.)

**38. 元 (Origin / Foundation)**
*   **Historical Origin:** It is a drawing of a person (the bottom legs) with a huge, emphasized head (the two horizontal lines at the top). Because the head is the top of the body, it evolved to mean the "start," "origin," or "source" of something. 
*   **Onyomi:** ゲン (gen), ガン (gan) | **Kunyomi:** もと (moto)
*   **N5 Words:** 元気 (げんき - healthy/spirit)
*   **Example:** お元気ですか。(おげんき ですか。 - How are you? / Are you well?)

**39. 見 (To See)**
*   **Historical Origin:** This is composed of a giant **目 (eye)** sitting on top of **儿 (legs)**. It is literally a walking eye! It represents active, intentional looking. 
*   **Onyomi:** ケン (ken) | **Kunyomi:** み.る (mi.ru), み.える (mi.eru), み.せる (mi.seru)
*   **N5 Words:** 見る (みる - to see/watch), 見せる (みせる - to show)
*   **Example:** テレビを見ます。(てれびを みます。 - I watch TV.)

**40. 口 (Mouth)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A simple, open square representing an open human mouth.
*   **Onyomi:** コウ (kou) | **Kunyomi:** くち (kuchi), ぐち (guchi)
*   **N5 Words:** 口 (くち - mouth), 出口 (でぐち - exit), 入口 (いりぐち - entrance)
*   **Example:** 口を開けてください。(くちを あけて ください。 - Please open your mouth.)

**41. 食 (To Eat)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top part (亼) represents a lid or covering. The bottom part (皀) is an ancient food vessel/bowl. It is literally a covered pot of food.
*   **Onyomi:** ショク (shoku) | **Kunyomi:** た.べる (ta.beru)
*   **N5 Words:** 食べる (たべる - to eat), 食べ物 (たべもの - food), 食堂 (しょくどう - dining hall)
*   **Example:** 朝ご飯を食べます。(あさごはんを たべます。 - I eat breakfast.)

**42. 飲 (To Drink)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **食 (food/eat)**. The right side is **欠 (a person yawning or opening their mouth)**. Opening your mouth to take in nourishment other than solid food = drinking.
*   **Onyomi:** イン (in) | **Kunyomi:** の.む (no.mu)
*   **N5 Words:** 飲む (のむ - to drink), 飲み物 (のみもの - drink/beverage)
*   **Example:** 水を飲みます。(みずを のみます。 - I drink water.)

### Time, Movement, and Directions

**43. 今 (Now)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (亼) means "to gather" or "contain," and the bottom is a little mark. It represents grabbing or pinpointing the current, fleeting moment.
*   **Onyomi:** コン (kon) | **Kunyomi:** いま (ima)
*   **N5 Words:** 今 (いま - now), 今日 (きょう - today), 今月 (こんげつ - this month)
*   **Example:** 今、何時ですか。(いま、なんじ ですか。 - What time is it now?)

**44. 行 (To Go)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look closely at this one. It is a drawing of a **four-way intersection or crossroad**. If you stand at a crossroad, you have to *go* somewhere. (This character is also a radical in many words related to walking or streets).
*   **Onyomi:** コウ (kou), ギョウ (gyou) | **Kunyomi:** い.く (i.ku), ゆ.く (yu.ku), おこな.う (okona.u)
*   **N5 Words:** 行く (いく - to go), 銀行 (ぎんこう - bank), 旅行 (りょこう - travel)
*   **Example:** 学校へ行きます。(がっこうへ いきます。 - I go to school.)

**45. 出 (To Exit / Leave)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Think of a plant sprouting. The bottom is a receptacle or hole (凵), and the top (屮) is a plant pushing its way *out* of the earth.
*   **Onyomi:** シュツ (shutsu) | **Kunyomi:** で.る (de.ru), だ.す (da.su)
*   **N5 Words:** 出る (でる - to exit/leave), 出口 (でぐち - exit), 出す (だす - to take out/submit)
*   **Example:** 部屋を出ます。(へやを でます。 - I leave the room.)

**46. 分 (Minute / To Divide)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **八 (to divide/split)**, and the bottom is **刀 (a knife)**. A knife physically slicing something apart. Time is "divided" into minutes. 
*   **Onyomi:** フン (fun), ブン (bun) | **Kunyomi:** わ.かる (wa.karu), わ.ける (wa.keru)
*   **N5 Words:** 五分 (ごふん - 5 minutes), 半分 (はんぶん - half), 分かる (わかる - to understand = *literally, to break things down in your mind*)
*   **Example:** 日本語が分かります。(にほんごが わかります。 - I understand Japanese.)

**47. 東 (East)**
*   **Historical Origin:** An incredibly poetic compound. It is **木 (tree)** superimposed with **日 (sun)**. The sun rising through the branches of a tree represents the East (where the sun rises).
*   **Onyomi:** トウ (tou) | **Kunyomi:** ひがし (higashi)
*   **N5 Words:** 東 (ひがし - east), 東京 (とうきょう - Tokyo = "Eastern Capital")
*   **Example:** 太陽は東から出ます。(たいようは ひがしから でます。 - The sun rises from the east.)

**48. 西 (West)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a bird (the inner strokes) returning to its nest (the outer box). Birds return to roost at sunset, which happens in the West.
*   **Onyomi:** セイ (sei), サイ (sai) | **Kunyomi:** にし (nishi)
*   **N5 Words:** 西 (にし - west)
*   **Example:** 西に山があります。(にしに やまが あります。 - There is a mountain in the west.)

**49. 南 (South)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally a drawing of an ancient bronze bell or wind chime hanging in a pavilion, which caught the warm, southern winds. 
*   **Onyomi:** ナン (nan) | **Kunyomi:** みなみ (minami)
*   **N5 Words:** 南 (みなみ - south)
*   **Example:** 南へ行きます。(みなみへ いきます。 - I will go south.)

**50. 北 (North)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A drawing of two people sitting back-to-back. Why North? In the Northern Hemisphere, houses were built facing South to get the sun. Your *back* was turned to the cold Northern winds. (Fun fact: This kanji is also the root for the word "to betray / turn one's back on," 背く).
*   **Onyomi:** ホク (hoku) | **Kunyomi:** きた (kita)
*   **N5 Words:** 北 (きた - north)
*   **Example:** 北は寒いです。(きたは さむい です。 - The north is cold.)

**51. 右 (Right)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (ナ) is an ancient drawing of a **hand**. Below it is **口 (mouth)**. Historically, the right hand was considered the dominant hand used to bring food to the mouth. 
*   **Onyomi:** ユウ (yuu), ウ (u) | **Kunyomi:** みぎ (migi)
*   **N5 Words:** 右 (みぎ - right)
*   **Example:** 右に曲がります。(みぎに まがります。 - Turn right.)

**52. 左 (Left)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (ナ) is again a **hand**. Below it is **工 (a tool or square used by carpenters)**. While the right hand did the active moving/eating, the left hand was the supporting hand that firmly held the tool! 
*   **Onyomi:** サ (sa) | **Kunyomi:** ひだり (hidari)
*   **N5 Words:** 左 (ひだり - left)
*   **Example:** 左を見てください。(ひだりを みて ください。 - Please look to the left.)

***
**Brain Teaser for a fast learner:** Notice how **分 (to divide)** is a knife cutting something, and **分かる (to understand)** uses the exact same kanji? In both Japanese and English, logic is about "breaking things down" or "analyzing" (which in Greek literally means to loosen/break apart).

###  Education & Size
Look closely at how many of the "Actions" use tools (axes, doors) and how many "Society" words trace back to ancient community structures

**53. 先 (Ahead / Previous)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top part is a variant of "foot/to stop" (止), and the bottom is "person" (儿). It depicts a person literally walking ahead of you. 
*   **Onyomi:** セン (sen) | **Kunyomi:** さき (saki)
*   **N5 Words:** 先生 (せんせい - teacher), 先月 (せんげつ - last month)

**54. 生 (Life / Birth)**

*   **Onyomi:** セイ (sei), ショウ (shou) | **Kunyomi:** い.きる (i.kiru), う.まれる (u.mareru), なま (nama)

**55. 大 (Big)**

*   **Onyomi:** ダイ (dai), タイ (tai) | **Kunyomi:** おお.きい (oo.kii)

**56. 小 (Small)**

*   **N5 Words:** 小さい (ちいさい - small), 小学校 (しょうがっこう - elementary school)
*   **Example:** あの猫は小さいです。(あの ねこは ちいさい です。 - That cat is small.)

**57. 学 (Study / Learning)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top part represents two hands interacting with a roof/building. The bottom is **子 (child)**. It shows a child absorbing knowledge inside a school building.
*   **Onyomi:** ガク (gaku) | **Kunyomi:** まな.ぶ (mana.bu)
*   **N5 Words:** 学生 (がくせい - student), 学校 (がっこう - school)

**58. 校 (School Building)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **木 (tree/wood)**, and the right is **交 (to intersect/mix)**. It refers to a wooden building where people (students and teachers) intersect and interact.
*   **Onyomi:** コウ (kou) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 学校 (がっこう - school), 高校 (こうこう - high school)

**59. 高 (High / Tall / Expensive)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A picture of a tall watchtower or a multi-story pavilion. The box in the middle is the upper window, and the box at the bottom is the main door. 
*   **Onyomi:** コウ (kou) | **Kunyomi:** たか.い (taka.i)
*   **N5 Words:** 高い (たかい - tall/expensive), 高校 (こうこう - high school)
*   **Example:** この本は高いです。(この ほんは たかい です。 - This book is expensive.)

**60. 文 (Sentence / Literature / Culture)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally a drawing of a person with a tattoo on their chest. Tattoos were "patterns," which evolved conceptually into written patterns (characters), and finally into literature and culture.
*   **Onyomi:** ブン (bun), モン (mon) | **Kunyomi:** ふみ (fumi - non N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 作文 (さくぶん - essay)
*   **Example:** 作文を書きます。(さくぶんを かきます。 - I write an essay.)

**61. 語 (Word / Language)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **言 (speech)**. The right side is **吾 (I / myself)**. Therefore, language is "my speech" or the words we speak to one another.
*   **Onyomi:** ゴ (go) | **Kunyomi:** かた.る (kata.ru - N4/N3)
*   **N5 Words:** 日本語 (にほんご - Japanese), 英語 (えいご - English)
*   **Example:** 日本語を話します。(にほんごを はなします。 - I speak Japanese.)

###  Society, Family, & People

**62. 父 (Father)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A drawing of a hand holding a stone axe or a rod. In ancient patriarchal societies, the father held the tool of labor and the symbol of authority.
*   **Onyomi:** フ (fu) | **Kunyomi:** ちち (chichi), とう (tou)
*   **N5 Words:** 父 (ちち - my father), お父さん (おとうさん - someone else's father)
*   **Example:** 父は医者です。(ちちは いしゃ です。 - My father is a doctor.)

**63. 母 (Mother)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the kanji for woman (**女**). Now add two dots inside of it. These represent breasts, symbolizing nourishment and motherhood.
*   **Onyomi:** ボ (bo) | **Kunyomi:** はは (haha), かあ (kaa)
*   **N5 Words:** 母 (はは - my mother), お母さん (おかあさん - someone else's mother)
*   **Example:** 母は料理が上手です。(ははは りょうりが じょうず です。 - My mother is good at cooking.)

**64. 子 (Child)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a baby wrapped in swaddling clothes. The horizontal line represents the baby's two little arms sticking out!
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi), ス (su) | **Kunyomi:** こ (ko)
*   **N5 Words:** 子ども (こども - child), 女の子 (おんなのこ - girl)
*   **Example:** 子どもが遊んでいます。(こどもが あそんで います。 - The child is playing.)

**65. 外 (Outside)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **夕 (evening/moon)**. The right side is **卜 (divination/magic)**. In ancient times, reading turtle shells or making divinations under the night sky was done *outside*.
*   **Onyomi:** ガイ (gai), ゲ (ge) | **Kunyomi:** そと (soto)
*   **N5 Words:** 外 (そと - outside), 外国 (がいこく - foreign country)
*   **Example:** 外は寒いです。(そとは さむい です。 - It is cold outside.)

**66. 国 (Country)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The outer box **囗 (border/wall)** surrounds a **玉 (jewel/king)**. A country is a territory surrounded by a border, ruled by a king or holding great wealth.
*   **Onyomi:** コク (koku) | **Kunyomi:** くに (kuni)
*   **N5 Words:** 国 (くに - country), 外国人 (がいこくじん - foreigner)
*   **Example:** 私の国はアメリカです。(わたしの くには あめりか です。 - My country is America.)

**67. 京 (Capital)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A drawing of a towering, high-roofed building on a hill. Such impressive structures were only found in the capital city.
*   **Onyomi:** キョウ (kyou) | **Kunyomi:** (none at N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 東京 (とうきょう - Tokyo)
*   **Example:** 東京に住んでいます。(とうきょうに すんで います。 - I live in Tokyo.)

**68. 会 (To Meet / Gather)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (亼) is a lid. The bottom (云) was originally a cooking pot. It meant putting ingredients together in a pot. It evolved metaphorically into "people gathering together."
*   **Onyomi:** カイ (kai) | **Kunyomi:** あ.う (a.u)
*   **N5 Words:** 会う (あう - to meet), 会社 (かいしゃ - company)
*   **Example:** 友達に会います。(ともだちに あいます。 - I will meet a friend.)

**69. 社 (Company / Shrine)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **礻 (altar/spirit)** and the right is **土 (earth/soil)**. Originally, it meant a local shrine to the earth god. Because people gathered around the shrine, it became the word for "community" or a "company" of people.
*   **Onyomi:** シャ (sha) | **Kunyomi:** やしろ (yashiro - non N5)
*   **N5 Words:** 会社 (かいしゃ - company), 会社員 (かいしゃいん - company employee)
*   **Example:** 私は会社員です。(わたしは かいしゃいん です。 - I am an office worker.)

**70. 員 (Member)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **口 (mouth)** and the bottom is **貝 (shell/money)**! In ancient bureaucracies, counting the number of officials meant counting "mouths" that needed to be paid with "money" (shells). 
*   **Onyomi:** イン (in) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 会社員 (かいしゃいん - office worker), 駅員 (えきいん - station attendant)
*   **Example:** 彼は駅員です。(かれは えきいん です。 - He is a station attendant.)

###  Daily Actions

**71. 新 (New)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the components! The left side combines **立 (stand)** and **木 (tree)**. The right side is **斤 (an axe)**. Taking an axe to a standing tree to get *fresh, new* wood!
*   **Onyomi:** シン (shin) | **Kunyomi:** あたら.しい (atara.shii)
*   **N5 Words:** 新しい (あたらしい - new), 新聞 (しんぶん - newspaper)
*   **Example:** 新しい車を買いました。(あたらしい くるまを かいました。 - I bought a new car.)

**72. 聞 (To Listen / Hear)**
*   **Historical Origin:** This one is almost comedic. It is an **耳 (ear)** squeezed between two swinging doors of a **門 (gate)**. It literally depicts leaning your ear against a door to eavesdrop on what is outside!
*   **Onyomi:** ブン (bun), モン (mon) | **Kunyomi:** き.く (ki.ku), き.こえる (ki.koeru)
*   **N5 Words:** 聞く (きく - to hear/listen/ask), 新聞 (しんぶん - newspaper = *literally "new things you hear"*)
*   **Example:** 音楽を聞きます。(おんがくを ききます。 - I listen to music.)

**73. 作 (To Make)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **亻 (person)**. The right (乍) was an ancient tool for cutting branches. It shows a person actively working with a tool to create something.
*   **Onyomi:** サク (saku), サ (sa) | **Kunyomi:** つく.る (tsuku.ru)
*   **N5 Words:** 作る (つくる - to make), 作文 (さくぶん - essay)
*   **Example:** ケーキを作ります。(けーきを つくります。 - I make a cake.)

**74. 入 (To Enter)**
*   **Historical Origin:** An ideograph of an arrowhead or a wedge being driven downward into an object. It represents penetrating or entering.
*   **Onyomi:** ニュウ (nyuu) | **Kunyomi:** はい.る (hai.ru), い.れる (i.reru)
*   **N5 Words:** 入る (はいる - to enter), 入口 (いりぐち - entrance)
*   **Example:** 部屋に入ります。(へやに はいります。 - I enter the room.)

**75. 帰 (To Return)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side (⻖) is a mound or a wall. The right side has a broom (帚) and a footprint. It implies returning to your home base to sweep, clean, and settle down. 
*   **Onyomi:** キ (ki) | **Kunyomi:** かえ.る (kae.ru)
*   **N5 Words:** 帰る (かえる - to return)
*   **Example:** 六時に家へ帰ります。(ろくじに いえへ かえります。 - I will return home at 6 o'clock.)

**76. 毎 (Every)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is a plant sprouting. The bottom is **母 (mother)**. Because mothers can give birth to multiple generations, and plants sprout year after year, the combined concept became "abundant," and eventually "every" or "each."
*   **Onyomi:** マイ (mai) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 毎日 (まいにち - every day), 毎月 (まいつき - every month)
*   **Example:** 毎日水を飲みます。(まいにち みずを のみます。 - I drink water every day.)

**Brain Teaser:** Notice how "Newspaper" (新聞) is made of "New" (cutting a tree with an axe) and "Hear" (eavesdropping at a door)? A newspaper is literally *freshly gathered gossip/news*!

###  Work, Rest & Technology

**77. 仕 (To serve / Work)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **亻 (person)**. The right is **士 (scholar/warrior/gentleman)**. It represents a person who serves in an official capacity or works as a professional retainer. 
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi), ジ (ji) | **Kunyomi:** つか.える (tsuka.eru)
*   **N5 Words:** 仕事 (しごと - work/job)
*   **Example:** 私の仕事は先生です。(わたしの しごとは せんせい です。 - My job is a teacher.)

**78. 事 (Matter / Thing / Fact)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A picture of a hand holding a flag or a writing brush over an object. It represents recording official matters or government affairs. Over time, it came to mean any abstract "thing" or "event."
*   **Onyomi:** ジ (ji) | **Kunyomi:** こと (koto)
*   **N5 Words:** 仕事 (しごと - work/job), 食事 (しょくじ - meal)
*   **Example:** 大変な事がありました。(たいへんな ことが ありました。 - A terrible thing happened.)

**79. 電 (Electricity)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the top half: **雨 (Rain)**. The bottom half is an ancient symbol for a **lightning bolt (申)** flashing from the sky. Rain + Lightning = Electricity!
*   **Onyomi:** デン (den) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 電車 (でんしゃ - train), 電話 (でんわ - telephone), 電気 (でんき - electricity/light)
*   **Example:** 電気を消してください。(でんきを けして ください。 - Please turn off the light.)

**80. 車 (Car / Vehicle)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A fantastic pictograph. Imagine looking down at a two-wheeled chariot from a bird's-eye view. The middle box is the carriage, with a wheel on top and bottom, and an axle running straight through the center.
*   **Onyomi:** シャ (sha) | **Kunyomi:** くるま (kuruma)
*   **N5 Words:** 車 (くるま - car), 電車 (でんしゃ - train = *electric vehicle*), 自転車 (じてんしゃ - bicycle)
*   **Example:** 車で学校へ行きます。(くるまで がっこうへ いきます。 - I go to school by car.)

**81. 休 (To Rest)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A masterpiece of logic. The left is **亻 (person)**. The right is **木 (tree)**. A person leaning against a tree to take a break from the hot sun.
*   **Onyomi:** キュウ (kyuu) | **Kunyomi:** やす.む (yasu.mu), やす.み (yasu.mi)
*   **N5 Words:** 休む (やすむ - to rest/take time off), 休み (やすみ - holiday/break)
*   **Example:** 日曜日は休みです。(にちようびは やすみ です。 - Sunday is a holiday.)

###  Communication & Thought

**82. 言 (To Say / Words)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The bottom is **口 (mouth)**. The horizontal lines above it represent a tongue moving or sound waves vibrating out of the mouth.
*   **Onyomi:** ゲン (gen), ゴン (gon) | **Kunyomi:** い.う (i.u), こと (koto)
*   **N5 Words:** 言う (いう - to say), 言葉 (ことば - word/language)
*   **Example:** 彼は「おはよう」と言いました。(かれは 「おはよう」と いいました。 - He said "Good morning.")

**83. 読 (To Read)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **言 (words/speech)**. The right side is a phonetic component that also meant "to sell/trade" (売). Originally, it meant to analyze or interpret the meaning of spoken words, which evolved into reading text aloud.
*   **Onyomi:** ドク (doku) | **Kunyomi:** よ.む (yo.mu)
*   **N5 Words:** 読む (よむ - to read)

**84. 思 (To Think)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The bottom is **心 (heart)**. The top (田) looks like a rice field, but anciently it was a drawing of a human brain/fontanel! Thinking is an action requiring both the brain (logic) and the heart (emotion).
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** おも.う (omo.u)
*   **N5 Words:** 思う (おもう - to think)
*   **Example:** 日本語は難しいと思います。(にほんごは むずかしいと おもいます。 - I think Japanese is difficult.)

**85. 話 (To Speak / Talk)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **言 (words)**. The right side is **舌 (tongue)**. Forming words with your tongue = talking!
*   **Onyomi:** ワ (wa) | **Kunyomi:** はな.す (hana.su), はなし (hanashi)
*   **N5 Words:** 話す (はなす - to speak), 電話 (でんわ - telephone = *electric speaking*)
*   **Example:** 友達と話します。(ともだちと はなします。 - I talk with my friend.)

###  Time, Space, & Conditions

**86. 次 (Next)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is "ice/cold breath" (冫). The right side is a yawning person (欠). Someone standing around breathing out cold sighs while waiting for their *next* turn!
*   **Onyomi:** ジ (ji), シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** つぎ (tsugi)
*   **N5 Words:** 次 (つぎ - next)
*   **Example:** 次の駅で降ります。(つぎの えきで おります。 - I will get off at the next station.)

**87. 何 (What)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **亻 (person)**. The right (可) meant carrying a load on a stick. It depicts a tired person carrying a heavy burden, asking, "What is it? / How much further?" 
*   **Onyomi:** カ (ka) | **Kunyomi:** なに (nani), なん (nan)
*   **N5 Words:** 何 (なに - what), 何時 (なんじ - what time)
*   **Example:** これはナンですか。(これは なんですか。 - What is this?)

**88. 午 (Noon)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally a drawing of a wooden pestle used to pound grain. It was borrowed phonetically to represent the "Horse" hour in the ancient Chinese zodiac, which happens to be exactly high noon.
*   **Onyomi:** ゴ (go) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 午前 (ごぜん - A.M. / Morning), 午後 (ごご - P.M. / Afternoon)
*   **Example:** 午後三時に会いましょう。(ごご さんじに あいましょう。 - Let's meet at 3 PM.)

**89. 後 (After / Behind)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side (彳) means a walking path. The top right (幺) is a thread. The bottom right (夂) is dragging a foot. Walking while dragging a foot behind you means you are lagging *behind* or coming *after*. 
*   **Onyomi:** ゴ (go), コウ (kou) | **Kunyomi:** あと (ato), うし.ろ (ushi.ro)
*   **N5 Words:** 後ろ (うしろ - behind/back), 午後 (ごご - P.M.), 後で (あとで - later)
*   **Example:** 家の後ろに木があります。(いえの うしろに きが あります。 - There is a tree behind the house.)

**90. 前 (Before / Front)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top was originally "a foot stepping forward" (止). The bottom right is **刂 (a knife)**. Moving your feet forward while using a knife to clear a path. You are cutting straight *ahead* or to the *front*.
*   **Onyomi:** ゼン (zen) | **Kunyomi:** まえ (mae)
*   **N5 Words:** 前 (まえ - before/front), 午前 (ごぜん - A.M.), 名前 (なまえ - name)
*   **Example:** 駅の前にいます。(えきの まえに います。 - I am in front of the station.)

**91. 間 (Between / Interval)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Very poetic! The outside is **門 (gate/doors)**. Inside is **日 (the sun)**. It is a picture of sunlight peeking through the *crack or interval* between a set of closed doors.
*   **Onyomi:** カン (kan), ケン (ken) | **Kunyomi:** あいだ (aida), ま (ma)
*   **N5 Words:** 時間 (じかん - time), 間 (あいだ - between)
*   **Example:** 銀行と郵便局の間にあります。(ぎんこうと ゆうびんきょくの あいだに あります。 - It is between the bank and the post office.)

###  Human Life & Concepts

**92. 名 (Name)**
*   **Historical Origin:** One of my favorites. The top is **夕 (evening/darkness)**. The bottom is **口 (mouth)**. In ancient times (before streetlights), if you bumped into someone in the dark, you couldn't see their face. You had to use your mouth to announce your *name*.
*   **Onyomi:** メイ (mei), ミョウ (myou) | **Kunyomi:** な (na)
*   **N5 Words:** 名前 (なまえ - name), 有名 (ゆうめい - famous)
*   **Example:** お名前は何ですか。(おなまえは なんですか。 - What is your name?)

**93. 白 (White)**
*   **Historical Origin:** It is a drawing of the **日 (sun)** with a spike of light (丿) bursting out of the top. The blazing sun is pure white light.
*   **Onyomi:** ハク (haku), ビャク (byaku) | **Kunyomi:** しろ.い (shiro.i), しろ (shiro)

**94. 雨 (Rain)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A literal pictograph of a weather system! The top horizontal line is the sky. The box is a cloud. The four inner dots are drops of water falling down.

**95. 書 (To Write)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top part (聿) is an ancient drawing of a hand holding a bamboo brush. The bottom is a pot of ink. A hand dipping a brush into ink to write!
*   **Onyomi:** ショ (sho) | **Kunyomi:** か.く (ka.ku)

**96. 友 (Friend)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The two strokes crossing each other (ナ and 又) originally represented two separate hands reaching out in the same direction. It is the ultimate symbol of two people holding hands or working together as friends.

**97. 家 (House / Home)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (宀) means a **roof**. The bottom (豕) is the kanji for a **pig/boar**. In ancient China, agriculture was central to life. Having a pig safely housed under your roof meant you had a wealthy, stable household. 
*   **Onyomi:** カ (ka), ケ (ke) | **Kunyomi:** いえ (ie), うち (uchi)

**98. 少 (Little / Few)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Look at the kanji for small (**小**). Now draw a long diagonal line slashing through it (**丿**). You are taking something small and cutting it to make it even *fewer*!
*   **Onyomi:** ショウ (shou) | **Kunyomi:** すこ.し (suko.shi), すく.ない (suku.nai)
*   **N5 Words:** 少し (すこし - a little)

**99. 古 (Old)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **十 (ten)**. The bottom is **口 (mouth)**. A story that has been passed down through *ten generations of mouths* is an incredibly old tale. 
*   **Onyomi:** コ (ko) | **Kunyomi:** ふる.い (furu.i)
*   **N5 Words:** 古い (ふるい - old)
*   **Example:** これは古い本です。(これは ふるい ほん です。 - This is an old book.)

**100. 知 (To Know)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **矢 (an arrow)**. The right is **口 (mouth)**. If you truly "know" something, you can speak of it as rapidly and accurately as an arrow striking a target.

###  Commerce, Movement & The Self

**101. 売 (To Sell)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top part (士) is a simplified version of **出 (to put out)**. The bottom part is a simplified **買 (to buy/shells)**. To "sell" literally meant putting your goods or money out into the open to make a trade!
*   **Onyomi:** バイ (bai) | **Kunyomi:** う.る (u.ru)
*   **N5 Words:** 売る (うる - to sell)
*   **Example:** あの店で靴を売っています。(あの みせで くつを うって います。 - They sell shoes at that store.)

**102. 買 (To Buy)**
*   **Historical Origin:** (We discussed this one!) It’s **罒 (a net)** swooping down to scoop up **貝 (cowrie shell money)**. Gathering up the ancient currency to purchase goods.
*   **Onyomi:** バイ (bai) | **Kunyomi:** か.う (ka.u)
*   **N5 Words:** 買う (かう - to buy), 買い物 (かいもの - shopping)
*   **Example:** 新しい服を買いました。(あたらしい ふくを かいました。 - I bought new clothes.)

**103. 自 (Self)**
*   **Historical Origin:** This is an absolute classic. It is a drawing of a human **nose**. In ancient China (and still in Japan today!), when someone wants to say "me" or "myself," they do not point to their chest—they point directly to their own nose!
*   **Onyomi:** ジ (ji), シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** みずか.ら (mizuka.ra)
*   **N5 Words:** 自分 (じぶん - oneself), 自転車 (じてんしゃ - bicycle = *self-turning-vehicle*)
*   **Example:** 自分で作りました。(じぶんで つくりました。 - I made it myself.)

**104. 立 (To Stand)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of a person standing on the ground (the bottom horizontal line) with their arms out and legs planted firmly.
*   **Onyomi:** リツ (ritsu) | **Kunyomi:** た.つ (ta.tsu)
*   **N5 Words:** 立つ (たつ - to stand)
*   **Example:** 立ってください。(たって ください。 - Please stand up.)

**105. 道 (Way / Road)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A deeply philosophical kanji. The left radical (辶) means "a walking path/movement." The right is **首 (a head/leader)**. A road is the path you take when you follow where the leader's head is pointing. Philosophically, this became *The Way* (Tao/Do)—as in Judo (The Gentle Way) or Kendo (The Sword Way).
*   **Onyomi:** ドウ (dou) | **Kunyomi:** みち (michi)
*   **N5 Words:** 道 (みち - road/street)
*   **Example:** この道をまっすぐ行きます。(この みちを まっすぐ いきます。 - Go straight down this street.)

**106. 町 (Town)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **田 (rice field)**. The right is **丁 (a street / city block)**. It represents agricultural fields that have been divided by streets into an organized, populated settlement.
*   **Onyomi:** チョウ (chou) | **Kunyomi:** まち (machi)
*   **N5 Words:** 町 (まち - town)
*   **Example:** 小さい町に住んでいます。(ちいさい まちに すんで います。 - I live in a small town.)

###  Time, Weather & Society

**107. 年 (Year)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Derived from **禾 (grain)** and **人 (person)**. It depicts a person carrying a heavy load of harvested grain on their back. Because the great harvest only happens once a full seasonal cycle, the "harvest" became the word for "year."
*   **Onyomi:** ネン (nen) | **Kunyomi:** とし (toshi)
*   **N5 Words:** 今年 (ことし - this year), 来年 (らいねん - next year)
*   **Example:** 今年は日本へ行きます。(ことしは にほんへ いきます。 - I will go to Japan this year.)

**108. 来 (To Come)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Originally, this was a drawing of a **wheat stalk**. Because wheat was not native to ancient China and was imported from the Middle East/Central Asia, it was known as "the grain that *came* from afar." The character was eventually borrowed to just mean "to come."
*   **Onyomi:** ライ (rai) | **Kunyomi:** く.る (ku.ru), き.ます (ki.masu), こ.ない (ko.nai)
*   **N5 Words:** 来る (くる - to come), 来年 (らいねん - next year)
*   **Example:** 友達が家に来ます。(ともだちが いえに きます。 - A friend is coming to my house.)

**109. 正 (Right / Correct)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **一 (a single line/target)**. The bottom is **止 (a foot)**. It depicts marching your feet straight toward a single, true target. Walking the straight and narrow path means you are "correct."
*   **Onyomi:** セイ (sei), ショウ (shou) | **Kunyomi:** ただ.しい (tada.shii)
*   **N5 Words:** 正しい (ただしい - correct/right), お正月 (おしょうがつ - New Year)
*   **Example:** あなたの答えは正しいです。(あなたの こたえは ただしい です。 - Your answer is correct.)

**110. 長 (Long / Leader)**
*   **Historical Origin:** A pictograph of an old man with very *long*, flowing hair, walking with a cane. Because long hair takes immense time to grow, it became the symbol for "long," and because elders were respected, it also means "leader" or "chief."
*   **Onyomi:** チョウ (chou) | **Kunyomi:** なが.い (naga.i)
*   **N5 Words:** 長い (ながい - long), 社長 (しゃちょう - company president)
*   **Example:** 彼女の髪は長いです。(かのじょの かみは ながい です。 - Her hair is long.)

**111. 住 (To Live / Reside)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **亻 (person)**. The right is **主 (master / burning lamp)**. A person who settles down like a stable, glowing lamp inside a house has taken up residence.
*   **Onyomi:** ジュウ (juu) | **Kunyomi:** す.む (su.mu)
*   **N5 Words:** 住む (すむ - to live/reside)
*   **Example:** 東京に住んでいます。(とうきょうに すんで います。 - I live in Tokyo.)

**112. 雪 (Snow)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **雨 (rain)**. The bottom (彐) is a picture of a **broom**. Snow is simply frozen rain that accumulates so much you have to sweep it away!
*   **Onyomi:** セツ (setsu) | **Kunyomi:** ゆき (yuki)
*   **N5 Words:** 雪 (ゆき - snow)
*   **Example:** 雪が降っています。(ゆきが ふって います。 - It is snowing.)

**117. 好 (To Like / Good)**

*   **Onyomi:** コウ (kou) | **Kunyomi:** す.き (su.ki)

**118. 手 (Hand)**

**120. 紙 (Paper)**

*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** かみ (kami), がみ (gami)

**121. 病 (Sick)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The outer shell (疒) is the "sickness radical"—a picture of a person shivering on a bed. The inside (丙) is a phonetic marker that originally meant "fire." A person shivering in bed with a burning fever. 
*   **Onyomi:** ビョウ (byou) | **Kunyomi:** やまい (yamai - non N5)

**122. 院 (Institution)**

*   **Onyomi:** イン (in) | **Kunyomi:** (none)
*   **N5 Words:** 病院 (びょういん - hospital)
*   **Example:** 病院へ行きます。(びょういんへ いきます。 - I am going to the hospital.)

**123. 映 (To Reflect / Project)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **日 (sun/light)**. The right is **央 (center)**. When light strikes the exact center of an object, it bounces back, creating a reflection or projection.
*   **Onyomi:** エイ (ei) | **Kunyomi:** うつ.る (utsu.ru)

**124. 画 (Picture / Stroke)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is a **brush/stylus**. The bottom is a **field (田)**. It originally meant drawing boundaries around agricultural fields, which evolved into the concept of drawing or painting pictures.
*   **Onyomi:** ガ (ga), カク (kaku) | **Kunyomi:** (none)

###  Places, Travel, & The Divine

**125. 市 (City / Market)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top (亠) is a lid or roof. The bottom (巾) is a towel, but historically it represented a **flag or banner**. In ancient times, markets were set up in open areas marked by a tall banner. Where the market goes, a city grows!
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** いち (ichi)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 市 (し - city), 市場 (いちば - market)

**126. 所 (Place)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **戸 (a door)**. The right is **斤 (an axe)**. Imagine taking an axe and clearing away brush or wood to build a door/shelter. You are carving out a specific "place" for yourself in the wild.
*   **Onyomi:** ショ (sho) | **Kunyomi:** ところ (tokoro)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 場所 (ばしょ - place), 近所 (きんじょ - neighborhood)

**127. 旅 (Travel)**

*   **N5/Genki Words:** 旅行 (りょこう - travel)

**128. 昔 (Ancient Times)**

**129. 神 (God / Spirit)**

###  Effort, Action, & Human Will

**130. 勉 (To make efforts)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Prepare yourself for this one. The left (免) means "to escape" or historically "to endure childbirth." The right is **力 (power/strength)**. Exerting massive physical strength to overcome a great, painful hardship. That is the ancient definition of "effort."
*   **Onyomi:** ベン (ben) | **Kunyomi:** つと.める (tsuto.meru)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 勉強 (べんきょう - study)

**131. 強 (Strong)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **弓 (a bow)**. The right (虫) means insect/bug, but originally referred to a strong, resilient silkworm. A bow that is thick, tough, and springy.
*   **Onyomi:** キョウ (kyou), ゴウ (gou) | **Kunyomi:** つよ.い (tsuyo.i)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 強い (つよい - strong), 勉強 (べんきょう - study = *literally: to force a strong effort!*)
*   **Example:** 毎日勉強します。(まいにち べんきょう します。 - I study every day.)

**132. 働 (To work)**
*   **Historical Origin:** This is a **Kokuji (国字)**—a kanji invented in Japan, not China! It perfectly combines **亻 (person)** and **動 (to move = heavy 重 + power 力)**. A person using power to move heavy things. Work!
*   **Onyomi:** ドウ (dou) | **Kunyomi:** はたら.く (hatara.ku)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 働く (はたらく - to work)
*   **Example:** 病院で働いています。(びょういんで はたらいて います。 - I work at a hospital.)

**133. 使 (To use)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left is **亻 (person)**. The right (吏) represents a government official holding a brush/tool. It originally meant to "send someone on an errand" or to "employ" someone to do a task. 
*   **Onyomi:** シ (shi) | **Kunyomi:** つか.う (tsuka.u)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 使う (つかう - to use)
*   **Example:** パソコンを使います。(ぱそこんを つかいます。 - I use a computer.)

**134. 有 (To exist / Have)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top left (𠂇) is an ancient drawing of a **hand**. The bottom (月) is actually a variant of 肉, meaning **flesh/meat**. A hand firmly grasping a piece of meat. In survival times, having food meant you possessed wealth, and therefore, you existed!
*   **Onyomi:** ユウ (yuu), ウ (u) | **Kunyomi:** あ.る (a.ru)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 有名 (ゆうめい - famous = *literally "having a name"*), 有る (ある - to exist/have *usually written in hiragana*)
*   **Example:** 彼は有名な先生です。(かれは ゆうめいな せんせい です。 - He is a famous teacher.)

**135. 起 (To get up / Wake)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left side is **走 (to run)**. The right side is **己 (self)**. Using your own willpower to lift yourself up and start moving.
*   **Onyomi:** キ (ki) | **Kunyomi:** お.きる (o.kiru), お.こす (o.kosu)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 起きる (おきる - to wake up)

**136. 早 (Early)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **日 (sun)**. The bottom (十) is an ancient helmet/armor. A soldier putting on his helmet right as the sun comes up to get an *early* start.
*   **Onyomi:** ソウ (sou) | **Kunyomi:** はや.い (haya.i)

###  Connecting, Separating, & Nature

**140. 牛 (Cow)**

*   **Onyomi:** ギュウ (gyuu) | **Kunyomi:** うし (ushi)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 牛乳 (ぎゅうにゅう - milk), 牛肉 (ぎゅうにく - beef)
*   **Example:** 牛乳を飲みます。(ぎゅうにゅうを のみます。 - I drink milk.)

**141. 歌 (To sing)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The left (哥) is two people chanting. The right (欠) is a person with a wide-open yawning mouth. Opening your mouth wide to belt out a tune!
*   **Onyomi:** カ (ka) | **Kunyomi:** うた (uta), うた.う (uta.u)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 歌 (うた - song), 歌う (うたう - to sing)
*   **Example:** 歌を歌います。(うたを うたいます。 - I sing a song.)

###  Colors & The "Bonus" Mark

**142. 赤 (Red)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **大 (big/a person)**. The bottom is **火 (fire)**. A person standing close to a massive, roaring fire, their skin flushed and glowing with the *red* heat.
*   **Onyomi:** セキ (seki) | **Kunyomi:** あか (aka), あか.い (aka.i)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 赤 (あか - red color), 赤い (あかい - red)
*   **Example:** 赤いペンがありますか。(あかい ぺんが ありますか。 - Do you have a red pen?)

**143. 青 (Blue / Green)**
*   **Historical Origin:** The top is **生 (sprouting plant)**. The bottom (丹) originally meant a well or minerals. A vibrant, fresh plant growing near clear water. In ancient Japanese/Chinese, "Ao" encompassed both the blue of the sky/sea and the vibrant green of nature. 
*   **Onyomi:** セイ (sei), ショウ (shou) | **Kunyomi:** あお (ao), あお.い (ao.i)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 青 (あお - blue color), 青い (あおい - blue)
*   **Example:** 空が青いです。(そらが あおい です。 - The sky is blue.)

**144. 色 (Color)**
*   **Historical Origin:** Anthropologically fascinating! It is a drawing of two people (the top ク and bottom 巴) bending over or kneeling intimately close to one another. Intimacy causes blood to rush to the face. The character originally meant "complexion" or "facial expression," which eventually broadened to mean all "color."
*   **Onyomi:** ショク (shoku), シキ (shiki) | **Kunyomi:** いろ (iro)
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 色 (いろ - color), 何色 (なにいろ - what color)
*   **Example:** 好きな色は何ですか。(すきな いろは なんですか。 - What is your favorite color?)

**145. 々 (Repetition Mark)**
*   **Historical Origin:** *Plot twist: This is not actually a Kanji!* It is a typographic symbol called a *Noma* (because it looks like Katakana 'no' ノ and 'ma' マ). It comes from a highly squiggled, cursive, shorthand version of the kanji 同 (which means "same"). It just tells you to repeat the kanji before it.
*   **Onyomi/Kunyomi:** N/A
*   **N5/Genki Words:** 時々 (ときどき - sometimes), 人々 (ひとびと - people)
*   **Example:** 時々、映画を見ます。(ときどき、えいがを みます。 - Sometimes, I watch movies.)
